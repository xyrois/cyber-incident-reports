# Cyber Incident Report: MGM Resorts International Ransomware Attack  
*(Written: February 2026)*

## Overview
In September 2023, MGM Resorts International, one of the world’s largest hospitality and casino operators, suffered a highly disruptive cyberattack that forced the shutdown of critical systems across its U.S. properties. The incident was attributed to a social engineering–enabled ransomware attack carried out by the ALPHV/BlackCat cybercriminal group, allegedly working with the Scattered Spider threat actor. The breach led to widespread operational outages, customer data exposure, and an estimated financial impact exceeding $100 million.

---

## Victim Organization
- **Name:** MGM Resorts International  
- **Sector:** Hospitality / Gaming / Entertainment  
- **Role:** Global casino and hotel operator with major properties in Las Vegas and across the United States

---

## Facts (What Happened?)
Attackers gained initial access to MGM’s internal systems through a **social engineering attack against an IT help desk employee**. By impersonating an employee, the attackers convinced support staff to reset credentials, granting them access without exploiting any software vulnerability.

Once inside, the attackers escalated privileges, moved laterally across MGM’s network, exfiltrated sensitive data, and deployed ransomware. MGM responded by proactively shutting down many systems, including hotel booking platforms, digital room keys, slot machines, ATMs, and point-of-sale systems, causing severe disruption to casino and hotel operations nationwide.

Exposed data reportedly included customer names, contact information, driver’s license numbers, passport numbers, and some Social Security numbers for a limited subset of customers.

---

## Timeline
- **September 10, 2023:** MGM detects unauthorized activity and shuts down systems across multiple properties  
- **September 11–12, 2023:** Widespread outages continue; guests report inability to access rooms, casinos, and payment systems  
- **September 13, 2023:** MGM confirms a cybersecurity incident involving customer data exposure  
- **September 14, 2023:** ALPHV/BlackCat claims responsibility for the attack  
- **October 2023:** MGM reports the incident will cost approximately $100 million in lost revenue and remediation expenses

---

## Motive
The primary motive was **financial extortion**. ALPHV/BlackCat is a ransomware-as-a-service (RaaS) group known for double-extortion tactics: stealing sensitive data before encrypting systems to pressure victims into paying ransom demands. MGM reportedly refused to pay the ransom, choosing instead to rebuild systems and restore operations independently.

---

## Damages & Impact
- **Operational impact:** Multi-day shutdown of hotel reservations, digital keys, casino systems, and payment infrastructure across MGM properties  
- **Data impact:** Personal data of customers exposed, including highly sensitive identification documents for some individuals  
- **Financial impact:** Estimated $100 million loss due to operational disruption, remediation, and cybersecurity response costs  
- **Reputational impact:** Significant public scrutiny regarding MGM’s internal security controls and employee security awareness

---

## Attribution
- **Threat actors:** ALPHV (BlackCat) ransomware group, allegedly collaborating with Scattered Spider  
- **Attack vector:** Social engineering attack targeting IT help desk procedures  
- **Attribution status:** Widely attributed by cybersecurity researchers and publicly claimed by the attackers

---

## Analysis & Opinion
This incident underscores a recurring weakness in enterprise security: **identity and access control failures amplified by human factors**. No zero-day exploit or advanced malware was required—only a convincing phone call. The breach highlights the necessity of strict help desk identity verification, mandatory MFA enforcement on privileged accounts, and real-time anomaly detection. Compared to breaches driven by software flaws or insider negligence, this case demonstrates how procedural gaps can be just as damaging as technical vulnerabilities.
