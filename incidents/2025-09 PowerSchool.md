# Cyber Incident Report: PowerSchool Data Breach (Sep 13, 2025)

## Overview
PowerSchool, a major education technology provider, suffered a data breach that exposed sensitive personal information belonging to more than 880,000 students and teachers across multiple Texas school districts. The incident was attributed to a single individual who gained administrative access due to missing basic security controls.

---

## Victim Organization
- **Name:** PowerSchool  
- **Industry:** Education Technology (Ed-Tech)  
- **Platforms:** Schoology, Naviance  
- **Scope:** K–12 education systems across multiple U.S. states

---

## Facts (What Happened?)
A 19-year-old attacker gained administrative access to PowerSchool’s systems, reportedly exploiting weak security practices such as the absence of multifactor authentication (MFA), inadequate encryption, and poor access controls. The breach exposed sensitive personal and potentially medical information belonging to students and teachers, particularly within Texas school districts.

Early warnings about security issues were reportedly raised months before the breach but were not adequately addressed.

---

## Timeline
- **February 2024:** A teacher reports cross-district access issues in Schoology, highlighting major security gaps  
- **Mid-2024:** Attacker gains administrative access to PowerSchool systems  
- **July 2024:** Texas Data Privacy and Security Act takes effect  
- **September 3, 2025:** Texas Attorney General files a lawsuit against PowerSchool  
- **September 2025:** Additional lawsuits filed in Illinois and Tennessee

---

## Motive
The likely motive was **financial gain**, given the exposure of Social Security numbers and medical information. A secondary motive may have been to expose PowerSchool’s lack of basic security controls, including missing MFA, weak encryption, and inadequate access restrictions.

---

## Damages & Impact
- **Individuals affected:** Over 880,000 students and teachers  
- **Data exposed:** Personally identifiable information (PII), including SSNs and potentially medical data  
- **Legal impact:** Major lawsuit filed by the Texas Attorney General; additional lawsuits in multiple states  
- **Risk exposure:** Ongoing risk of identity theft, fraud, and potential public safety concerns

---

## Attribution
- **Responsible party:** Individual attacker (19-year-old from Massachusetts)  
- **Affiliation:** No known ties to organized cybercrime groups  
- **Attribution status:** Confirmed individual actor

---

## Analysis & Opinion
This breach was largely preventable and demonstrates a failure to implement fundamental cybersecurity practices. Despite early warnings, PowerSchool did not deploy essential protections such as multifactor authentication or strong encryption. As a result, nearly one million students and educators are now exposed to serious long-term risks. This incident should serve as a wake-up call for the ed-tech industry and a turning point in how student data security is prioritized and regulated.
