# Cyber Incident Report: Askul Corporation RansomHouse Cyberattack (Nov 4, 2025)

## Overview
In October 2025, Askul Corporation, a major Japanese office and household goods retailer and logistics provider, suffered a cyberattack attributed to the ransomware group *RansomHouse*. The attackers claimed to have exfiltrated approximately 1.1 terabytes of data and disrupted Askul’s e-commerce and logistics operations, impacting both the company and its downstream business partners.

---

## Victim Organization
- **Name:** Askul Corporation  
- **Industry:** Retail, E-commerce, Logistics  
- **Region:** Japan  
- **Role:** Office and household goods retailer and logistics provider

---

## Facts (What Happened?)
Askul detected a ransomware-related intrusion in mid-October 2025. The group known as RansomHouse claimed responsibility and stated that it had exfiltrated approximately 1.1 TB of data from Askul’s systems. The stolen data reportedly included customer and supplier information, purchase histories, and contact and inquiry records associated with Askul’s online platforms.

In addition to data theft, the attack disrupted Askul’s logistics and e-commerce operations, affecting order fulfillment and shipments for both Askul and organizations that rely on its logistics services.

---

## Timeline
- **October 19, 2025 (approx.):** Askul detects ransomware infiltration  
- **October 31, 2025:** Askul publicly confirms a personal data leak involving names and email addresses  
- **Late October–November 2025:** Media reports on the incident as the investigation continues  
- **Ongoing:** Askul investigates the full scope and impact of the breach

---

## Motive
The attack appears to be motivated by **data extortion**. RansomHouse is known for a “steal-and-threaten” model, in which large volumes of data are exfiltrated and victims are pressured to pay ransom to prevent public release. The stolen customer, supplier, and purchase data holds significant value for extortion, resale, and reputational damage.

---

## Damages & Impact
- **Data exfiltration:** Approximately 1.1 TB of data claimed stolen  
- **Data types:** Customer and supplier information, purchase histories, contact and inquiry records  
- **Operational disruption:** E-commerce and logistics systems disrupted; shipment and order fulfillment delays reported  
- **Business impact:** Potential regulatory scrutiny and reputational harm

---

## Attribution
- **Claimed threat actor:** RansomHouse  
- **Threat actor profile:** Associated with Russian-aligned cyber operations  
- **Attribution status:** Unconfirmed by law enforcement at the time of reporting

---

## Analysis & Opinion
This incident illustrates how cyberattacks against logistics providers can cascade across multiple businesses and industries. The scale of data exfiltration reflects a broader shift toward extortion-based attacks that prioritize data theft over encryption alone. Organizations that operate or depend on third-party logistics providers should strengthen vendor security assessments, improve incident response coordination, and develop contingency plans for supply chain disruptions.
